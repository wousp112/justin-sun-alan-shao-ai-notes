# Reading notes: AI, work, and learning

These editorial notes organize Justin Sun’s conversation with Alan Shao (邵艾伦), [*对话孙宇晨：年轻人如何抓住AI时代的机会？*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY). They paraphrase the conversation. The section links lead to nearby discussions; themes recur across the interview.

## Give an agent context it can reuse

Sun describes feeding an AI tool observations from daily life, including photographs, training records, and sleep data. He uses files to retain the context and produce recurring reports. The useful distinction is between a single answer and a continuing record that another tool can also read.

His broader suggestion is to explain your circumstances to AI, keep the information available, and update it with outcomes. He also suggests comparing models and discussing disagreements with them.

His position on trust is forceful and uneven. At one point he says to trust AI completely and execute its advice. Elsewhere he retains a role for human judgment, debate, and correcting the information supplied to the model. Both positions belong to the conversation; they do not establish that AI answers are reliable.

[Watch the opening discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY&t=117s).

## Explore the field before choosing a path

Sun argues that a decision improves when you have seen enough alternatives to form a useful comparison. He uses house hunting and scouting a game map as examples. AI can help gather information before you commit money or time.

The interview includes an informal mathematical example about when to stop searching. These notes preserve the exploration principle without treating its spoken numbers as a verified decision rule.

He encourages people to combine interests and strengths with a wider view of demand. The opportunity may be outside their usual city, industry, or language. AI translation and research are discussed as ways to widen that search.

[Watch the opportunity discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY&t=1500s) · [Watch the decision examples](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY&t=2340s).

## Make information usable by machines

Several examples share one idea: information becomes more useful to an AI system when the system can read and work with it. Sun discusses digitizing records, organizing scientific data, expressing mathematical work in formal systems, and making payments programmable.

He mentions scientific research, health services, AI infrastructure, and crypto payment systems as possible areas of opportunity. These are his proposed directions, not a verified list of attractive markets or jobs.

He also gives an example of differences in access or costs across markets. That example includes a condition of lawful use. It should not be read as a current offer or permission to evade a service’s rules.

[Watch the discussion of machine-readable knowledge](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY&t=5580s).

## Keep room to move

Sun describes following opportunities where one can create value. He argues that large commitments can reduce that flexibility. The discussion includes how family arrangements can make a move difficult, even when a new opportunity looks attractive.

The implication is a tradeoff: a person’s choices depend on obligations as well as the apparent quality of an opportunity. His own freedom to move is not a description of everyone’s circumstances.

[Watch the mobility discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY&t=4920s).

## Update beliefs when the conditions change

Sun compares changing technology and business conditions to updates in a game. A strategy that worked under one set of rules may lose its advantage under another. He argues for learning the new conditions rather than treating past success as permanent proof.

He does not claim that AI will remain the best opportunity forever. He leaves room for later changes in technology and in the direction of his own business.

He also describes a “small ego”: less attachment to defending a successful identity, and more willingness to learn from unfamiliar people or ideas. The practical test in the conversation is whether new information changes behavior.

His claim that intelligence is only a matter of information is much stronger than that practical test. It is recorded here as his opinion, not as an established psychological finding.

## Treat health as part of the ability to work

Sun gives physical health a central role in a long working life. He discusses proactive records and feedback rather than waiting for a problem to become urgent. He describes how AI discussions changed some of his own views about exercise.

The notes retain that role for health. Personal measurements, supplement doses, and detailed prescriptions are omitted. The interview is not evidence for a particular medical or training regimen.

[Watch the health discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY&t=3000s).

## Learn from specific strengths

Sun identifies Elon Musk, Warren Buffett, and Donald Trump as different reference points for engineering, finance, and public influence. His account concerns the traits he chooses to study. It does not require agreement with everything a reference figure says or does.

He also tells the host that a content career and an interest in investing do not have to become one combined business. Different strengths can coexist. His predictions about crypto assets remain investment opinions, not a portfolio template.

[Watch the discussion of separate strengths](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY&t=6900s) · [Watch the reference figures discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Z-vhBvBmUY&t=12840s).

## Gain experience, then create something from it

Sun frames life as an expanding set of experiences. New perspectives can change what a person wants. He describes wanting firsthand experience as an employee because much of his own experience has been as a founder.

He argues for creating more and consuming less, while recognizing a role for rest and recovery. Experience can become writing, products, research, or other work. AI can help express it, but the conversation places value on the original experience supplied by a person.

He leaves marriage and some questions of meaning unresolved. Those passages express uncertainty rather than a universal formula for life. His preference is to start exploring a broad direction without demanding a complete explanation of its eventual meaning.

## Use the notes to ask a better question

The following questions are an editorial application of the interview:

- What does my agent need to know about my actual situation?
- Which alternatives have I compared, and which have I missed?
- What useful output could I make before committing further?
- Which obligation limits my options?
- What evidence would change my current belief?

[AGENT.md](AGENT.md) turns these questions into a reusable decision prompt. Its suggested tests and output format are created for this project; they are not instructions quoted from Sun or Shao.
